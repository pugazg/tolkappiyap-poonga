# Blossom 2 — Vowels and Consonants · continued

“The eighteen sacred steps of Lord Sabarimalai Ayyappa shrine would remind me of the eighteen consonants, sir”, was the reply of student Maṇivācakaṉ.

Pūṅkōti got up to speak wisely, “The great Cēra king Ceṅkuṭṭuvaṉ vanquished the Aryan kings Kanaka Vicayar at Kuyilāluvam to get the stone for carving Kaṇṇaki’s statue. He made them carry the stones on their heads. At that juncture, an elated Ceṅkuṭṭuvaṉ roared, ‘The war between gods and demons lasted for eighteen years. The great Rāmāyaṇa war spanned out for eighteen months. The Mahābārata war went on for eighteen days, whereas Cēraṉ Ceṅkuṭṭuvaṉ’s war against the Himalayan kingdom finished within eighteen nāḻikai (one nāḻikai = 24 minutes).’

Since I remember this inspirational speech, the fact that consonants are eighteen would remain deeply embedded in my mind.”

The teacher patted Pūṅkōti proudly and concluded, “Though based on spirituality and rationality, which are poles apart, your answers are appropriate.”

The teacher conveyed his greetings to the students.

***

> The twelve phonemes (from /a/ through /au/)  
> Are known to be vowels,  
> So has it been laid down.
>
> (*Eḻuttatikāram : Nūl Marapu*, verse 8)

> The eighteen phonemes (from /k/ through /ṉ/)  
> Are called consonants,  
> Thus goes the usage codified.
>
> (*Nūl Marapu*, verse 9)
