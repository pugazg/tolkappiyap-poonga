# Blossom 25 — That is like a Mother! · continued

My finely-jewelled girl—nourished by me since infancy, through breast-feeding and intake of jackfruit, banana, mango, and millet mixed with honey—remained under my shadow till attaining puberty, but eloped with an abject novice like cotton popping out of its seed flying away. Friend, shouldn’t we consult the auguries and omens for the wellbeing of the one who left me (though I protected her like a couch shell) and the one who led her away from me? Shouldn’t we seek divine soothsaying? Shouldn’t we take vows to the *mullai*’s presiding deity Kaṇṇaṉ? Wonder what might happen to them on the way! Wonder what might be their condition right now! Wonder what sort of problems might await them in the future!

Thus, instead of getting furious with the eloped lovers, the kind-hearted mother is murmuring her concern that they should not be haunted by any evil, whomso-ever she has gone with:

> As the heroine goes on elopement with the hero,  
> The utterances appropriate to her mother are:  
> She would pour out her anguished concern  
> To the confidante and to those who had seen the lovers on the way;  
> She would speak  
> Referring to predictions unsought and divinations and prognostications,  
> Weighing the good and the bad that might befall the lovers,  
> Expressing apprehensions about the path they trod  
> And about the help that might come their way,  
> Reflecting on the past, the now and the future of her daughter.  
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Akattiṇai iyal*, verse 36).
