# Blossom 37 — The Concept of Victory

We can achieve victory by either vying with others or by striving on our own. *Vākai* does not refer only to the victory achieved in war. In fact, it is the attaining of a lofty goal in life through virtuous means.

*Tolkāppiyam, Poruḷatikāram* explains the classifications of *vākai* as well as its defining traits and its grammar.

That verse in question begins thus, “The sixfold occupations of the brahmins,” After reading it, one might feel like wondering, “What exactly is this Brahmin occupation? How on earth did brahmins appear in *Tolkāppiyam*?”

They had indeed arrived but their arrival was portended in *Collatikāram* itself. Somehow, the Tamiḻs had lost sight of the fact that the sound of the bell would signal the advent of the elephant.

In the cultivated fields weeds grow along with crop unannounced. *Tolkāppiyam* touches upon that in the verse below:

> *Vaṭacol* comprises words (borrowed from Sanskrit)  
> That steer clear of the system of sounds in Sanskrit  
> And that come to be adapted to the native form and sound.
>
> (*Collatikāram : Ecca iyal*, verse 5)
