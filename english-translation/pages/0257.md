# Blossom 92 — The Explanation of Veḷḷaivāraṇar · continued

Wherever due, Veḷḷaivāraṇar does not fail to deny the existence of caste system in those days. He shows in his scholarly and objective study *Literary History of Tolkāppiyat Tamiḻ* that the social castes was a much later development in Tamilnāṭu. He observes:

> Tolkāppiyam Marapiyal verses 71-85 that deal with four castes are the interpolations of a later period.
>
> Tolkāppiyar classifies people in terms of the landscape divisions, and not on the basis of their colour (varṇam) anywhere in his treatment of the living modes of people in the earlier sections. The Marapiyal section deals with only the conventions vogue in his day relating to male-female distinction and young ones (vide verses 1-70 and 86-90). Verses 71-85 on castes represent a break in this order being added later.
>
> Where Tolkāppiyar classifies people in terms of their Akam love conduct and in terms of their Puṟam conduct in vākaittiṇai, the classifications are based on division of land and types of occupation, and he does not designate them by caste as Vēḷāḷar or Vaiciyar. And the term vaicikaṉ does not occur anywhere in Caṅkam literature. Given that Tolkāppiyar does not speak of four castes, it is incorrect to say that he has referred to kings and traders as the intermediate class (iṭaiyiru vakaiyōr). Besides, the interpretation (of verses 77 and 82) that the other two castes have no martial traits runs counter to the life portrayed in Caṅkam works. Tolkāppiyar does not mention vēḷāṉ (peasant) people as a caste. Such of these references in Tolkāppiyam are proper to Akam and Puṟam themes and do not fit into the section on conventions.
>
> Therefore, it is to be clearly understood that these verses 71-85 must have been included in the text only after the caste system took roots on the Tamiḻ soil at a much later period; that they have been wrongly put into Tolkāppiyam when alien rulers like Kaḷappirar held sway in Tamilnāṭu trampling down the native Tamil consciousness and the Tamil mores; and that commentators like Iḷampūraṇar and Pērāciriyar misinterpreted Tolkāppiyam caught helplessly as they were in the milieu of their days when the northern fourfold caste system nurtured by Sanskrit writers like Manu slowly entered into the social fabric of Tamilnāṭu.

(Veḷḷaivāraṇar, *Tolkāppiyat Tamiḻ Ilakkiya Varalāṟu : Marapiyal Viḷakkam*  
(The Literary History of Tolkāppiyat Tamil: Conventions of Poetry))
