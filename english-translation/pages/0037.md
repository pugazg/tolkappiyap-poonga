# Blossom 3 — Consonant-vowels · continued

> But can my frail heart  
> bear your loss added!
>
> ......  
> ......
>
> I know, it is conspiracy of Nature,  
> Aṇṇā .... you!
>
> Till the time I join you in  
> the abode you repose in,  
> kindly lend me your mighty heart, Aṇṇā!  
> I swear to present it at your lotus feet  
> When I join you, Aṇṇā.

Beads of tears swept my cheeks even when I was reciting the verse. On observing my melancholy, my friends tried to divert my attention by broaching sundry stories. At last, they came to square one.

A friend noted, “Just observe the peach of a gift Tamil has presented us. Aṇṇā with ‘a’ as the first letter in his name was our leader. Now it is Kalaiñar Karunanidhi with ‘ka’ as the first letter in his name.”

I intervened, “Sir, I am just the consonant /k/. It becomes the consonant-vowel ‘ka’ only when it combines with the vowel /a/ that begins Aṇṇā’s name.”

***

> A consonant-vowel is said  
> With vowel following the consonant.
>
> (*Eḻuttatikāram : Nūl Marapu*, verse 18)
