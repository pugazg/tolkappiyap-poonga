# Blossom 40 — The Tamiḻ Concept of Love · continued

> Is what is called *piracāpattiyam*;  
> *Āriṭam* is taking a girl in marriage  
> Along with the gift of cattle;  
> Giving the girl’s hand to a performer of sacrifice  
> Before the sacrificial fire is *teyvam*;  
> *Gandharvam* is the union of love  
> Between a man and a woman running into each other;  
> *Acuram* is giving the girl learned to one adept in archery  
> Who tames a killer bull  
> And who is after gifts of wealth;  
> *Irākkatam* is coercive marriage of a girl by a boy:  
> And *paicācam* is union with a girl  
> Older by age, given to sleep and drink.
>
> Tolkāppiyar identifies these forms  
> As those of the Vedic Aryan culture  
> And rejects them subtly  
> And they do not go with the Tamiḻ mores,  
> So says our respected Tamiḻ scholar Veḷḷaivāraṇar.
>
> *Kaḷavu* is theft, stealing small things;  
> It is quite natural that it is despised as an act of wrong kind.
>
> But *kaḷavu* as a union of love --  
> Between a man and a woman  
> Who are united by the union of hearts  
> With no hurry for physical intimacy,  
> Who do not transgress the bounds of propriety  
> And who meet clandestinely  
> Aided by their kindly companions,  
> Whose bond of union courses through  
> Ways of virtue and love  
> Combined with codes of worldly needs  
> The bond uniting the two as one
