# Blossom 31 — Peruntiṇai

*Maṭalēṟutal*, the public practice of men riding on the horse made of dried palmyra leaves that cause bruises all over the body with blood oozing, by which to compel the parents of the girl to give her in marriage to him; Men and women in the evening of their life, being filled with abnormal desire to enjoy union, which assails them like a poniard;

The extreme of carnal desire remaining unbridled like a truant child, in spite of all attempts to restrain it; and

The violent pursuit of passion that surges up like a turbulent sea -

All these four are acts of *peruntiṇai* (abnormal sexual passion) and they are not totally alien to those of *kaikkiḷai*.

The practice of riding on the palmyra horse is not confined to the front of the house of the hero. In fact, the man comes to the street and all through the village. This act would persist until the parents of the heroine consent to hand the girl to him. Where it does not happen, the man would threaten to give up his life.

Once the man and woman are sapped of youth, they should contemplate activities to be undertaken for the welfare of the society and the future generation. They should continue their service to humanity by realizing that sex is not an end in itself, taking the cue from the placement of the section on sexual love in *Tirukkuṟaḷ* where Tiruvalluvar implies that sex must end at a point in life, and should not persist as an end in life.

Owing to unbridled sexual desire and by giving vent to their carnal cravings, certain folks plunge into socially ostracized activities like murder and rape. They push themselves into these socially degrading deeds, without fully realizing their enormity.
