# Blossom 72 — Four Sources of Laughter · continued

Do you think that the Pāṇṭiya kingdom is a place of recluses?  
No, no, it is not so.  
Our presiding deity Mother Pāṇṭi has not begotten cowards.

Oh! Countrymen! Virtuous souls! Trust me.  
This young Neṭuñceḻiyaṉ would add to the fame of our motherland  
Thus did the young king instil valour into his subjects.

The foes looked down upon the youth and mocked him.  
He in turn laughed at their arrogance and it was he who had the last laugh.

## Stupidity and Credulity

A person preferred a written complaint to the police station about the theft of a metal pot (*tavalai*)

The police official peered through and burst out laughing, “But then your head is very much intact.”

Owing to poor knowledge, he missed the letter ‘va’ and has instead written *talai* in his complaint.

Just as the official explained, he also joined in the laughter, got back his petition and made the correction.

The official laughed again and enquired,  
“Pray, from which well did the frog disappear?”

Again, instead of *tavalai* he had mistakenly written *tavaḷai* meaning frog.

So ignorant was he that after having made the pot a head, he now has made it a frog.

These four are then situations and objects that have the potential to provoke laughter. Here is the *Tolkāppiyam* verse:

> The four sources of laughter are  
> Ridicule, childishness, stupidity and credulity.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Meyppāṭṭiyal*, verse 4)
