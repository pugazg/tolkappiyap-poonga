# Blossom 48 — The Aiyar Conceived the Marriage · continued

“All is fine!  
No need for worry!  
The clandestine love career has come to an end.  
Let’s conduct the marriage called *karaṇam*  
That would be a celebration  
Of the love of these two.”

***

> In so far as  
> The love career of the hero and the heroine  
> Unfolds to be inferred in discourses private,  
> And in so far as  
> It is thrown into the subject of gossip in public,  
> The acts of the hero must bear the prime responsibility for it.
>
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Kaḷaviyal*, verse 48)
>
> The hero and the heroine  
> Enter into marital union  
> Either after their clandestine union becomes public knowledge  
> Or before it happens.
>
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Kaḷaviyal*, verse 49)
>
> Sage men there conceived solemnized wedding  
> When falsity and breach of trust cropped up,  
> So has it been said.
>
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Karpiyal*, verse 4)

Note: *Aiyar* is an indigenous Tamiḻ word meaning a respectable elderly person of superior virtue and worth. Here this term refers to one of Tamiḻ antiquity. There are misinterpretations of Aiyar as being derived from ‘Aryan.’ The Aiyar formulated the marriage rites to guard against love falling into deceit and falsity. This phase of marriage had been preceded by a culture when they did not visualize the need for marriage. It is obvious that the conduct of marriage should have come into existence before the days of Tolkāppiyar. The need for marriage then might have been necessitated after the advent of the alien cultures and races.

- Veḷḷaivāraṇar  
  *The History of Tolkāppiyam.*
