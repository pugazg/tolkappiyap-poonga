# Blossom 19 — Land and Time · continued

> The wee hours just before daybreak  
> Are assigned to the cultivable tract.  
> The day’s close before sunset  
> Belongs to the littoral tract.
>
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Akattiṇai iyal*, verse 8)

> As we see it,  
> The hot season and midday hour  
> Go with the centrally placed [arid] tract.
>
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Akattiṇai iyal*, verse 9)

> The last dewy season  
> Is also held to be proper to the arid tract.
>
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Akattiṇai iyal*, verse 10)

After having described all these, Tolkāppiyar shows that it is inevitable that the nature of love behaviour may change, and not the nature of land and its characteristics.

> That  
> Aspects of love behaviour  
> Assigned to a given tract  
> Do get mixed with those of another tract  
> Is no deviation unacceptable;  
> Yet,  
> No commingling is permitted among the tracts themselves,  
> So goes the norm by men of discernment.
>
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Akattiṇai iyal*, verse 12)
