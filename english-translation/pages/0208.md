# Blossom 68 — Semantic Shift · continued

When we call a person jealous or compare that person to Gandhari (Duriyodhana’s mother in *Mahābhārata*) we are using the term *poṟāmai* (envy, jealousy) in its derogatory sense.

But when the film *Parācakti* scripted by me became a roaring success, my good friend poet Kannadasan visited me. After hugging me, he exclaimed, “Sir, I feel really jealous of you.” He kissed me on the forehead. In this context the word parts with its negative sense to become something endearing and appreciative. Thus the word develops a special sense.

Poverty is not acceptable in any form. Eradicate poverty. This is the war-cry of progressive people and the vow of those in governance. Poverty no doubt is a cruel thing.

“I withered every time I chanced upon withered crops.” These are the words of Vaṭalūr Vaḷḷalār Rāmaliṅka Aṭikaḷār, a saint who had a great compassion for the poor and the downtrodden. As in the case of the two words discussed above, the word *vaṟumai* (poverty) too undergoes a kind of regeneration through Vaḷḷalār comparing it to withered crops.

Anger, ignorance, jealousy, and poverty are to be avoided by us. Be that as it may, there are contexts where they have a positive worth.

> The four traits of the hero and heroine--  
> Anger, ignorance, envy and penury --  
> Do occur in them  
> As attributes (implied) of their positive worth.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Poruḷiyal*, verse 51)
