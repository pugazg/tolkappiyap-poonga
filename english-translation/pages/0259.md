This classification apart, Tolkāppiyar identifies the beings that are endowed with each of these six classes of faculties. That the ancient Tamils have written the grammar of life on the earth on scientific basis just amazes us.

The division of living things into six categories on the basis of the five senses and the sense organs has been done in terms of their hierarchy of intelligence and power - which demonstrates the scientific knowledge and scientific perception of phenomena of the ancient Tamils. This knowledge and perception constitute the core of the grammar of life propounded in Tolkāppiyam.

Look at the following verses:

> The grass and tree are single-sense organisms;  
> Other species of the flora there are too  
> That are limited to this faculty.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Marapiyal*, verse 28)

> The snail and shell-fish are two-sense organisms;  
> Others of their species there are too.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Marapiyal*, verse 29)

These verses show that grass and tree are one-sense beings using the faculty of the body. Be it noted that Tolkāppiyar states that there are other species too that are one-sense organisms. Such of these instances show Tolkāppiyam to be a treasure-house of scientific knowledge and information.

Look at the following verses where Tolkāppiyam gives examples of the beings that are endowed with two, three, four and five faculties.

> The snail and shell-fish are two-sense organisms;  
> Others of their species there are too.  
> The termite and ant are three-sense organisms;  
> There are others of their kind confined to these faculties.  
> The crabs and beetles are four-sense organisms;  
> Others of their species there are too.  
> The beasts and men sans discrimination  
> Are creatures with five senses;  
> There are co-sharers among their species too.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Marapiyal*, verses 29, 30, 31, 32)
