# Blossom 92 — The Explanation of Veḷḷaivāraṇar

Veḷḷaivāraṇar observes:

> Conventions regarding the organization of morphemes and prosody apart, conventions vis-a-vis the male and female species and the offspring of human and non-human classes, conventions on the fourfold social classes of the human world, conventions on grass and trees, conventions as regards the features of the physical world and literary conventions - all these come under ‘Conventions’.

I shall discuss Veḷḷaivāraṇar’s views on the social classes later in this essay.

A look at Tolkāppiyar’s description of male and female species and the offspring of the human and non-human classes shows that they have gone through extensive changes in worldly usage.

> Kuṭṭi is the offspring designation of  
> Mongoose, wild cat, rat and three-striped squirrel.

(*Marapiyal*, verse 6)

This verse states that the young ones of mongoose, wild cat, rat and squirrel are called by the name *kuṭṭi*. But in usage today, there are no such designations as *kirikkuṭṭi* or *aṇilkuṭṭi*; instead, they are called *kirippiḷḷai, aṇilkuñcu*, etc. The young of cat alone is called *pūnaikkuṭṭi*. This changed usage applies to other designations in Tolkāppiyam like *narippiḷḷai, paṉṟippiḷḷai*, etc. which the commentators too have pointed out.
