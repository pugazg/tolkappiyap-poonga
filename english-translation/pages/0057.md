# Blossom 12 — Word-picture

The lover urged his beloved, “My dear, you have received coveted awards in the art of painting. Why don’t you draw us in an embracing posture and put it in a place not frequented by others, so that both of us can attain ecstasy by viewing it?”

“I will certainly draw such a picture. You are a sculptor. Kindly chisel a statue depicting the posture you mentioned.”

“Painting first; statue next.”

The lady gracefully obliged her lover. Later, she led him to the retiring room and showed him the painting.

It was a poetic depiction of a lover embracing his love. The tight hug revealed only one of her breasts. Of the two eyes, one of the eyes was concealed by the cheek of the lover which was brushing against her cheek. Her unbridled locks were spread all over the shoulders of the lover like the jasmine creeper, which brought only one of his shoulders to the fore. The painting showed her other shoulder being hugged tightly by his hands.

“You have painted by adhering to the grammatical norms”, was the crisp reaction of the lover. He further elucidated, “Of the two eyes, only one eye is visible in the painting. In the same manner, the painting doesn’t unravel two bosoms; only one of them is visible. You have represented your shoulders also in a similar way. I am able to view only one eye, one shoulder, one breast, instead of two eyes, two shoulders and two breasts. In a nutshell, the painting is a true reflection of *Tolkāppiyam*’s grammatical norm.”
