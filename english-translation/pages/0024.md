# Preface · continued

*Tolkāppiyam*, Caṅkam works, *Cilappatikāram*, *Maṇimēkalai*, *Tirukkuṟaḷ*, etc. the fact remains that *Tolkāppiyam* has not received due attention from the Tamil people.

Dr Tamilannal, a respected Tamil scholar, has expressed this concern in his foreword to S.V. Subramanian's prose paraphrase of *Tolkāppiyam*. The latter was a professor of Tamil, Kerala University, whose services to Tamil need to be commended. His linguistic studies, following in the footsteps of the eminent Tamil scholar V. I. Subramanian, deserve a significant mention.

Aided by this work and other commentaries of scholars, research findings and scholarly articles available, I entered into the garden of *Tolkāppiyam* with confidence.

The section on Letters of the Alphabet (*Eḻuttatikāram*), in *Tolkāppiyam* is like jackfruit with a rugged, thorny outer surface, but will not prick and cause bruises.

The formation of words requires letters too; phonemes alone will not do. The string of words goes into the making of *Poruḷatikāram*, the grammar of meaning.

For one to enjoy the pulp-like *Poruḷatikāram*, one needs to go through *Eḻuttatikāram* which can be described as the protective outer cover of *Poruḷatikāram*.

Once we succeed in going past these two components of letters and words and the meanings they carry, there is the delicious pulp called grammar soaked in literature.

S.V. Subramanian contends that Tolkāppiyar has brought in ‘Letter’ and ‘Word’ as an aid to treating ‘Meaning and the Grammar of Literature’. This section titled *Poruḷatikāram* gives a synthetic account of the process of evolution, the classification of living things, the modes of living of the humans, their life at home (*Akam*) and their societal life (*Puṟam*), the attributes and characteristics of men and women, things to be eschewed, etc. Broadly speaking, *Tolkāppiyam* is a study of the development of the human species.

Looked at from the present-day perceptions of social order, it is possible to assume that the pre-Caṅkam Tamil life had been dotted by things regressive. Questions of acceptability of such a life may also arise. Even though this ancient culture contains elements of living modes, the rights and liberties of men and women, etc., that need to be corrected and bettered, what wrong does it entail for one to seek to learn about the bases of a culture that existed several thousand years ago, just as one evinces scholarly interest in archaeological studies?

We build dams across rivers, we erect temples. Even though these structures, being built in tune with the modern technology, impress us with their stately appearance, can they make the Big Temple built by Raja Raja Cōḻaṉ at Tanjavur about a thousand years ago and the Kallaṇai (dam built of stone) constructed by Karikāl Cōḻaṉ obsolescent?
