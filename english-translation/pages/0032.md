# Blossom 1 — Letters of the Alphabet · continued

All the primary letters of the alphabet prostrated before him and stood in a row in order:

*a, ā, i, ī, u, ū, e, ē, ai, o, ō, au* formed the head of the row.

They were followed by *k, ṅ, c, ñ, ṭ, ṇ, t, n, p, m, y, r, l, v, ḻ, ḷ, ṟ, ṉ*.

At that juncture, a shade appeared near the wall which was followed by yet another shade. Since the sounds raised by these two shades were low, Tolkāppiyar realized the difference and opened his eyes wide. One of the shades identified itself as *ikaram*. Another shade introduced itself as *ukaram*. Tolkāppiyar looked at them and pronounced strictly that they would be a part of the secondary letters taking the names *kuṟṟiyalikaram* and *kuṟṟiyalukaram*. They could not be placed among the first thirty primary letters.

At that time, the figure of a new letter clenching a stick (*āytam*) appeared and roared, “Do you agree to put me among these thirty?”

Tolkāppiyar retorted promptly, “What, do you threaten me? Just because you appear with a weapon, do you think I will shiver with fear and allot a place for you in the first row?”

Realizing Tolkāppiyar’s wrath, the ‘āytam’ letter said meekly, and with all humility, “Sir! Kindly put me in any place as you wish. Even if I don’t get placed in the front row, I will offer my assistance whenever a need arises.”

Tolkāppiyar smiled in jest after writing the first verse and said, “You are offering assistance! Quite funny.”

The āytam letter said ecstatically, “See how talented I am! You have given me a chance in your very first verse.”

Tolkāppiyar read his verse again:

> Thirty are the phonemes  
> From /a/ through /ṉ/;  
> Excluding the three, the secondary ones,  
> So has it been handed down.
>
> (*Eḻuttatikāram : Nūl Marapu*, verse 1)

He was surprised to note that the āytam letter has found a place in the word *muppaḵtu* (thirty) of his first verse.

The thirty letters of the Tamil alphabet thus begin with ‘a’ and end with ‘ṉ’.
