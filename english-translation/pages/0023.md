# Preface · continued

> Endowed with a tongue of virtuous words  
> And in the wisdom of the four vedas rooted;  
> He expounded in terms irreproachable  
> The structure unambiguous of the language,  
> And thereby,  
> The sage of the man that he was  
> And of renown exceeding marked,  
> Established in the sea-girt world  
> His name Tolkāppiyaṉ  
> Versed in the treatise *Aintiram*.

As we read this prologue - that indicates that the Tamil land of that day extended from Vēṅkaṭam in the north to Kumari in the south, and that records for posterity that *Tolkāppiyam*, the grammar of ‘letters, words and meaning’, composed by Tolkāppiyar well-versed in the treatise *Aintiram*, was presented in the court of Nilantaru Tiruvil Pāṇṭiyaṉ before the learned presided over by Ataṅkōṭṭācāṉ - we experience the relish of jackfruit.

I mentioned in the preface to my commentary on the complete text of *Tirukkuṟaḷ* that I had ventured to cover the Himalayas with a shawl..

And now, I desired to enter into the flower-garden of *Tolkāppiyam*, go around in it with diligence and care and make a selection of a hundred blossoms and make them the object of my enjoyment. This I did with extreme hesitation as does a damsel entering a flower garden and plucking as many flowers as her tresses would hold even though she desired to pluck all the flowers in the garden and deck her head with them.

Even though I wrote commentaries on all the 1330 couplets, I chose to write my verbal portraits on about 400 couplets only. In a similar way, I look at selecting from the garden of *Tolkāppiyam* some of the richly grown blossoms and present them here.

The readers might ask, “Are you going to give us what you have relished for yourself?” But this is the country where even God is said to have accepted the used flower gift of Nācciyār. The readers may therefore bear with my act, for, after all, my medium is only paper unlike the case of Nācciyār.

During the medieval period, thanks to the great resurgence of interest in literature, the ancient Tamil works began to be read by people. Scholars like Iḷampūraṇar, Nacciṉārkkiniyar, Cēṉāvaraiyar, Kallāṭar and others not only preserved the *Tolkāppiyam* text but wrote commentaries on it as well. The lamp lit by them burnt brightly.
