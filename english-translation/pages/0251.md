# Blossom 89 — Here is the Fifth Source · continued

The tempers became frayed which ended up in a big ruckus.

A person from a nearby house approached them and asked, “What men, why are you howling like foxes?”

Now, both these men joined together to take up cudgels against the third person. Fortunately, a grammar teacher who happened to pass by pacified them and explained:

> Making similes using the donkey, dog and fox is an instance of the fifth  
> basis of simile referred to in the Uvamaiyiyal. This is done to make clear the  
> inferiority of a particular thing. It is natural to expose its characteristics  
> through such similes. Hence these are called trivial things.

Though they could not comprehend what he was stating, they could grasp that he was pacifying them through some good advice. They stopped shouting and the quarrel ended.

Thus we see that the sources of a simile come to five when a thing of lowly signification is added.
