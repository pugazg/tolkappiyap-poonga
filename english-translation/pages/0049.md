# Blossom 8 — Honour · continued

“What role are the brother and sister going to play in that dance?”

“Nallatampi would don the role of Kōvalaṉ, while his sister pairs opposite to him as Kōvalaṉ’s mistress Mātavi.”

“Brother and sister playing the characters of Kōvalaṉ and Mātavi?” exclaimed Cellaiyā, the scholar. After a momentary pondering, he commented laughingly, “māṉamillai” meaning ‘dishonourable’.

It was enough to provoke the parents, “Teacher! How dare you say such a defaming and offending word to us!”. The agitated parents lost no time to arm themselves with a sickle and a log.

It sent shivers down the spine of the teacher who muttered, “Why such agitation? Now, what offensive thing did I utter?”

“Then what? You enquired about my children and dismissed them as shameless! How can I tolerate this?”

“Well, I am just returning from the school after teaching *Tolkāppiyam* to children. Since my mind was still preoccupied with what I taught in the classroom, I stammered something. In retrospect, there is nothing wrong with my remark.”

“What? Is there nothing wrong?”

“Kindly bear with me! Let me offer my explanation first. In *Tolkāppiyam*’s *Eḻuttatikāram* there is a verse on *cāriyai*:

> For the intervening empty morpheme *iṉ* to occur  
> After the two preceding terms *āṭūu* and *makaṭūu*  
> [Besides the hard consonant augmentation]  
> Is no failing in use.

*Cāriyai* doesn’t mean dance alone; it also refers to morphemes of closed word class. The relevant verse states clearly that in ‘*āṭūu*’ and ‘*makaṭūu*’ which refer to man and woman respectively, not only hard consonants but even if *cāriyai* occurs, it is not wrong.

‘*āṭūu*’ and ‘*makaṭūu*’, that is, brother and sister playing the roles of Kōvalaṉ and Mātavi respectively is not erroneous. This is what I wished to state. *Māṉam* means wrong, *māṉam illai* means that it is not wrong.”

The parents sighed with relief and looked at the teacher, “Sir, though brother and sister are paired opposite to each other, it is after all a form of art. Therefore, it is not a sin. Didn’t you get a simpler word to express the same opinion? Why did you perplex us by uttering the seemingly offending word ‘*māṉam illai*’?”
