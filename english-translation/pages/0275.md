# Blossom 99 — The Lamp on the Hill

If we were to establish a garden, we should decide which trees and flowers should adorn it, think of and implement the amenities needed for the comfort of those who come for a stroll and also carefully avoid things which should not find a place in the garden.

Since the establishment of a garden can have parallels with the making of a book, a verse in Tolkāppiyam throws light on the defects to be avoided in a book. Traffic rules prescribe whether we should keep to the right or the left though it differs from country to country. Although this is an important issue, even more important is the fact that without obtaining the permission or observing the signal one should not cross the road.

In the same manner, though a swimming pool is essential in a park, one should not dig a deep well in another corner of the park. The reason is that children who come to the park, accompanied by their parents, should not fall into the well by accident.

A few would come to the garden holding a book with the intention of reading it there. For their sake, bright lamps should burn in a few places. In the same way, lamps should not be there in places where men and women can relax enjoying privacy which is necessary after a day’s work. If light is required there it should be dim.

Though the toilet is essential to answer the call of nature, it should not be constructed in the centre of the park.
