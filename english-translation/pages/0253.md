# Blossom 90 — The Crow and the Peacock · continued

The national Tamil poet Bharati sings, “Hey, Lord Krishna, I can see your pleasant dark colour on the wings of the crow!” The colour black on the crow’s feather is not used for a simile here but we witness it being presented in a positive light.

The expression, “cascading tresses” should not be interpreted as representing grey hair. It must be understood that it represents only thick and long hair.

In the phrase, “eyes-like black beetle”, we don’t get apprehensive that the eyes might have legs like the beetle’s or that they fly. It would only demean the importance of the simile.

One cannot use a simile indiscriminately.

This opinion of Veḷḷaivāraṇar is worth remembering.
