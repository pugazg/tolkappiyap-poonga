# Blossom 26 — Great is the Aid of the Confidante

*Poruḷatikāram* codifies the responsibilities of the heroine’s confidante, when the hero prepares himself to take leave of the heroine. “How is it fair on your part to start off for attaining either a personal or social goal leaving your lady alone? Just as fish cannot live without water, it is illogical to think that she would live without you. You better take her also with you”, requests the confidante.

In the meantime, she tries to console and get ready the heroine, who sheds tears for departing from her near and dear relatives. But, all of a sudden the friend has a doubt.

Even though he seems to love her truly, on that eventful day, in the garden of the Kaṇṇaṉ temple he fetched a seemingly inaccessible fruit for another damsel and smiled meaningfully at her, directing an equally mischievous question, “I have got that fruit. Would I get these fruits?” Shouldn’t she recognize his true colours from his appearance of being an embodiment of truth and falsehood? Thus ponders the friend. But there comes a change of heart.

While thinking about the lover and the heroine’s mother, the friend is reminded of the Kaṇṇaṉ temple incident. But she has to focus upon the plight of a disconsolate, grief-stricken, pining heroine.
