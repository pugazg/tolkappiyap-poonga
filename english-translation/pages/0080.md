# Blossom 21 — Land and People: A Study in Excellence · continued

> Pursuit of learning  
> And ambassadorial mission  
> Are proper to men of superior social classes.  
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Akattiṇai iyal*, verse 26)

In both these activities, men of excellence and character took the responsibility.

Tolkāppiyar says that it is the prerogative of the king to either go on war himself, or entrust the responsibility to his commanders.

> Going on war himself against his enemy  
> And entrusting the job  
> To those associated with him  
> Are given to the king.  
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Akattiṇai iyal*, verse 27)

The implication is that the duties of governance, fighting, learning and ambassadorship were assigned to men of character, intelligence and valour. Further, in all types of land, there were people who excelled in the performance of specific tasks and duties.

> Conduct of prayers and festivities  
> For those of abiding note other than the humans (i.e. deities);  
> Restoration of righteousness where it turns deviant  
> In all regions extending through the forest tract;  
> And to make riches through virtue abroad -  
> Are occasions too  
> For the hero to remain parted from the heroine.  
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Akattiṇai iyal*, verse 28)

Therefore, the performance of duties proper to a given type of land goes to the nobility, the inhabitants of the land, men from lower strata and workers.

> The conduct of prayers and festivities  
> Stands enjoined on the inhabitants of the four regions.  
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Akattiṇai iyal*, verse 29)

A few commentators misread ‘the four types of people’ as ‘four castes of people.’

That the classification of men meant for the management of the given type of land was misinterpreted as a classification on the basis of caste is but the product of perverted thinking.
