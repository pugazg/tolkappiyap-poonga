# Preface · continued

Urged by such a line of thinking, I invite you to enter into the flower garden of *Tolkāppiyam*.

Observes Ilakkuvanar, one of the fighters for the cause of Tamil and a professor of Tamil, in his *Tolkāppiyam* studies:

> Among the extant works of ancient Tamil, *Tolkāppiyam* alone is of aid to us in studying the ancient Tamil life in its varied manifestations. But it is being learned only by the students of Tamil for purposes of examination. Although it is a work of grammar, it is not like the grammatical texts in other languages. It embodies in itself current advances in linguistics and literary research being pursued with scholarly interest today in the West, as also studies in biology, psychology and in the art of living.  
> ... Therefore, let us study the grammars of letters, words, literature and life through Tolkāppiyar and establish the greatness of the Tamil land.

Next to Ilakkuvanar, the scholar who impressed me by his scholarship in Tamil is V. Sp. Manickam, who describes *Tolkāppiyam* as the soul of Tamil life.

Scholars are of the opinion that neither in the Indian languages nor in the other languages of the world had there witnessed a work like *Tolkāppiyam* during that era. There might have been books in these languages, but not one of the magnitude of *Tolkāppiyam*.

We can describe *Tolkāppiyam*, that predates all extant works in Tamil, as one like sugar cane that needs to be bitten and relished, and as a heap of honeyed fruits that lies handy for us to use and be benefitted from.

Bowing my head in the direction where lie Pērariñar Aṇṇā and the revolutionary poet Pāratitācaṉ in eternal sleep who encouraged me in my endeavours to acquire scholarship in Tamil;

Expressing my gratitude to the Tamil scholar M. Varadarajan who inspirited me by his kindly words that after Nalaṅkiḷḷi and Neṭuṅkiḷḷi it is I who reign over the land and the letters;

And remembering my Tamil teachers Mahavidwan Dhandapani Desikar, Muttukiruṭṭiṇa Nāṭṭar, Dr S. Ilakkuvanar and Rajagopal Piḷḷai,

I make bold to approach the *Eḻuttu* (the letters of the alphabet) first. I invite you to join me graciously to relish the fragrance of the blossoms of *Col* (words and their organization) and *Poruḷ* (the grammar of literature and life).

September 1, 2002

**Sincerely,**  
**(M. Karunanidhi)**
