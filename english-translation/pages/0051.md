# Blossom 9 — Cōḻaṉ

Ten years ago, an old woman was seen staggering with a stick on the bank of the Koḷḷiṭam river, being unable to walk with an upright gait due to scorching heat. A cowherd youth sitting under a tree took pity upon the ageing woman and approached her with some fruits. The youth held her famished hands and enquired, “Grandmother! Where are you going in this hot sun? You are hardly able to walk. Here, have these fruits.” He offered the blackberries to her.

The old woman scrutinized the fruits carefully and remarked rather mockingly, “Are these fruits hot or cold?”

“The aged don’t lose their sense of humour, do they? I am no Lord Murukaṉ. First, have these fruits!”

The old woman tasted the fruits, touched the shoulders of the youth affectionately and questioned, “Son! Why are you rearing cows at this age instead of going to school?”.

“Well! I am studying. I do this work only during holidays.”

“Is there nobody at home?”

“My father is afflicted with tuberculosis. When I go to school, my mother rears the cows and milks them to make our both ends meet.”

“It is quite pathetic! How do you concentrate upon your education, amid such gruelling poverty? What are you studying and what do you want to become?”

“I am studying Tamil. My ambition is to work as a Tamil teacher!”

“Is it so? Quite good! Is your Tamil teacher tutoring you in literature?”

“Oh yes! He is teaching *Tirukkuṟaḷ, Tolkāppiyam* and all.”
