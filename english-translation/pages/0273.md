# Blossom 98 — Primary and Secondary Works · continued

> These four modes are:  
> Summarization, elaboration,  
> Synthesis of summarization and elaboration  
> And translation in conformity with the tradition of the receptor tongue.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Marapiyal*, verse 98)

The explanations of these four verses are as follows:

(1) Given the fact that the events of popular speech draw on the use of language by the literate, what is called linguistic usage is the usage of the literate. This apart, this usage defines and informs the worldly usage, poetic usage and the normal, grammatical usage. Following the literate usage and simplifying the poetic usage and the worldly usage for the people at large to access it will go a long way in the development and protection of the language.

(2) The present work gave me the opportunity to engage myself in the protection and preservation of the Tamil language.

(3) When it comes to the meaning of verse 95 given above, a misinterpretation will give room for assumptions and questions like

• Tolkāppiyam is the primary work.  
• It was composed by a person of consummate wisdom.  
• He was also the one of unblemished mind.  
• Will Tolkāppiyar go to describe himself in such terms?

Primary works are those that precede and that form the basis or the source of secondary works.

Dr Ilakkuvanar in his *Study of Tolkāppiyam* observes as follows:

> It is said that Tolkāppiyam is the earliest extant work in Tamil. No other work predating Tolkāppiyam has come to us. It does not mean that no work was written before Tolkāppiyam. Tolkāppiyam is a work of grammar. In any language, literature precedes grammar. Tamil is no exception to this. Here too grammatical works came to be written only after the advent of literary works. Tolkāppiyam was written after not only works of literature but also grammatical treatises, which is attested to by Tolkāppiyam itself. Therefore, Tolkāppiyam must be seen as the earliest extant work and not the first, primary work.
