The sun is scorching. On the way, she is encountered by some sage men treading the desert path. She discloses her predicament to them. “Have you seen my daughter along with a broad-shouldered man?” so saying she trembles in anxiety.

“We did see them, mother. The flag of love flies aloft in their hearts. Don’t think of cutting it off, mother.”

“What are you saying? When her own mother and me, her foster-mother, are grieving thus, is it proper for you to support the girl who has gone away to our grief?”

“Mother, listen! Though sweet-smelling sandal sprouts and grows in the mountain, it doesn’t belong to the mountain. It is meant for applying on the body. Being born in water, can the pearl remain wedded to the water? It will add to the beauty of the one who wears it. The music emanating from the *yāḻ* gives pleasure not to the *yāḻ* but to the auditors or the one who plays it. Mother, your daughter is like sandal born from the mountain, pearl born from the sea, music from the *yāḻ*. Therefore, it is best for her to be with her loved man.” Here is that *Kalittokai* poem:
