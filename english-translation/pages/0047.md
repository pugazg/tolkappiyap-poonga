# Blossom 7 — Intrusive Consonant · continued

“That I possess in abundance - but then Cupid’s arrow burns me like fire.”

“Get away! Don’t speak irrationally!”

“Well, you only taught...?”

“What did I teach? Speak up.”

“ ‘If a word ends with a vowel to be followed by another word beginning with a vowel, *uṭampaṭumei* (intrusive consonant) would ensue; that form would not be disapproved by anybody but rather accepted’. This is what you taught with the example *mā + ilai → māvilai*. Just have a look at us. Aren’t we a fine example of this phenomenon? When grammar endorses coalescing of *uyir* (vowels), why cannot it be extended to our bodies?”

“You stupid girl! Are you trying to put into practice the lesson taught? I spoke of the grammar of letters but you extend it to my neck.”

“Don’t worry, dear! I have learned my lessons in the right sense. Was there anything wrong in my understanding of *Tolkāppiyam*?”

“It was impeccable, but is this the place to practise it?”

“Oh! Pardon me, If place is what bothers you, there is a hall nearby.”

She dragged him and he followed her to the hall.

***

> Where the preceding word’s final  
> And the succeeding word’s beginning  
> Are both vowels,  
> The intervention of an intrusive consonant [/y/, /v/]  
> Is not forbidden.
>
> (*Eḻuttatikāram : Puṇariyal*, verse 38)
