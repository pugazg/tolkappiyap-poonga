# Blossom 86 — Know Your Simile · continued

Tolkāppiyar in the verse quoted above speaks of the classification of similes.

This classification comprises (i) comparison of things that are accessible to the eyes, and (ii) comparison of things that are accessible to other sense organs or to the mind. We shall illustrate these classes and the grounds for comparison.

• She flew off as a sparrow (comparison based on deed)  
• munificent like rain (comparison based on effect)  
• neck like a conch (comparison based on form)  
• gold-like complexion (comparison based on colour)

Look at the following examples:

• flute’s like speech (through hearing)  
• sweet like honey (through taste)  
• scorching like the summer sun (through bodily feeling)  
• fragrant breeze (through smell)  
• O lovely moon!  
  Is the joy of your sight  
  Like the joy of sighting  
  A potful of white rice  
  While looking for  
  A little gruel in hunger? (through the mind)

Apart from these that we have drawn on the first verse of *Tolkāppiyam : Uvamaiyiyal*, the second verse speaks of the mixed categories of similitude:

> The grounds for comparison above  
> Do get intermingled too.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Uvamaiyiyal*, verse 2)
