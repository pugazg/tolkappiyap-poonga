# Blossom 8 — Honour

Explaining grammar is not my sole objective in this work. It is an attempt at aiding learners of *Tolkāppiyam* to retain in mind the significant aspects of grammatical norms and principles. As a part of this, I seek to explain here *cāriyai* (empty morph) which unfolds in the form of a scene.

Wordbooks define *aikāram* as chest, ornament worn on the shoulder, rebuke, snake, falsehood, elephant’s food, etc.

In the same manner, *māṉam* means self-respect, haughtiness, pain, guilt, love, affection and chastity.

The grammatical term *cāriyai* is not only a word of the closed system but also a kind of dance of the *puṟam* mode.

*āṭūu* denotes a male and *makaṭūu* denotes a female.

Bearing these in mind, it would be useful as well as amusing to read this ‘blossom’.

Now, Nallatampi and Nallataṅkai - brother and sister - were taking leave of their parents for staging a dance performance.

A learned Tamil teacher - Cellaiyā, marked with a confused mind, met the siblings on the way. On encountering their parents, the teacher once again enquired, “Where are your children off to?”, completely forgetting his meeting with the children earlier.

“Isn’t today āṭipperukku? The rivers would flow to their fill marking the greatness of the regime. In such a joyous function, which men and women enjoy to the hilt, my son and daughter are going to enjoy themselves by entertaining the people through a dance”, was the explanation offered to the teacher.
