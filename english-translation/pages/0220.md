# Blossom 73 — Four Sources of Grief · continued

As a burning torch of ethnic pride,  
As a billhook felling things disgraceful,  
As an Ingarsal refuting the very divinity,  
As a Socrates who never minced words,  
As a Voltaire in questioning things,  
Did our Periyār live his life!  
Behold him lying on the lap of death!  
He didn’t die young, a full life had he lived though  
If Taj Mahal were to be engulfed by the earth,  
Would we comfort ourselves saying, “Well, it was an old building?”  
Would we bear if the Big Temple of Tanjavur were to fall  
Saying it is an aged structure?  
Like these unforgettable monuments  
Periyār too has history made!

An emotion of this kind is what Tolkāppiyar calls decline (*acaivu*) that would cause grief and distress.

## Poverty (vaṟumai)

(Poverty had driven the *purāṇic* Nallataṅkāḷ and quite a number of mothers of the modern age as well to either sell the children begotten by them to eke out a living or drown them in a pond or river only to follow suit, later. The flooding tears at such spectacles of poverty constitute the sentiment of weeping.)

* * *

> The four inexorable sources of grief are  
> Contempt, deprivation, decline in stature and indigence.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Meyppāṭṭiyal*, verse 5)
