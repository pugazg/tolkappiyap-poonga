# Blossom 11 — Grammatical Renunciation

It was a mutt looking like a palace, adorned with a front hall that resembled Hari engrossed in deep sleep over the mythical *athiseshan* snake. A young ascetic was sitting on the veranda of that mutt looking forward to the arrival of somebody.

Not just that. His ears were keenly expecting the unlocking sound from the adjacent prayer room, while the eyes kept a vigil on the door of the room.

The door did not open. But then the Tamil teacher, teaching literature to the chief priest of the mutt, appeared well before the expected time. With the sacred Vaishnavite religious mark on his forehead and a book in his hand, the teacher came near the young monk.

“What Ciṉṉaccāmi (junior monk), how are you?” asked the teacher.

“Teacher, sir! I am the junior pontiff of this mutt. You are referring to me as if I were Kiḷappāḷuvūr Ciṉṉaccāmi who self-immolated.”

“Pardon me. He was but a lion-hearted Tamil.”

“Since I am keeping guard here, am I then an ugly Tamilian?”

“Okay, leave it. The nocturnal chic would have come. Have they fled off?”

“You, teacher! How come you ask of such things without any inhibition?”

“Why should I be afraid? Are the pontiffs devoted to God in deep sleep after exhaustion, or are they taking a rest? Is this an unbroken meditation? That is what I am asking.”

“That does not allow you to raise questions breaking the rules of grammar!”
