# Blossom 88 — The Sources of Similitude

> Pre-eminence, loveliness, love and might  
> Are the four characteristics  
> That abide as sources of similitude.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Uvamaiyiyal*, verse 4)

This verse shows that though the simile in a comparison is loftier than the subject, it has excellence, beauty, love and valour as the sources.

In this context, I would like to bring in a few illustrations from the historical novel *Rōmāpuri Pāṇṭiyaṉ* written by me.

In the beginning of the year 20 BC, one day, the city of Pūmpukār ruled by the Cōḻa king Karikālaṉ wore a festive look. The Cōḻa and the Pāṇṭiya flags fluttered together over the palace near the Maruvūrppākkam seashore of Pūmpukār. On that day Pāṇṭiyaṉ Peruvaḻuti and Cōḻaṉ Karikālaṉ met and established their friendship.

On that occasion, the poet Kārikkaṇṇanār sang in praise of the two monarchs:

> You are the cool-watered Cauvery’s lord, and he...

Starting thus, it goes on;

> With water and grain easily available to everybody,  
> The king reigns with a cool sceptre the Tamil-flourishing Maturai,  
> Where witness sandal from mountains,  
> Pearl from the sea and the resounding drum.
