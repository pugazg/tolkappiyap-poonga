# Blossom 90 — The Crow and the Peacock

> The vehicle and the tenor in similitude  
> Must be in accord with norms of propriety.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Uvamaiyiyal*, verse 8)

The simile and the thing which is compared must be comparable enough to make the listeners happy and accept them.

For example, in the expression *tuṭi iṭai* (slender waist) *tuṭi* means a small drum. It describes the slender waist placed in between the broad chest above and the broad lower abdomen below being compared to the drum *tuṭi*. Since the upper and lower parts of the body remain broad and the middle part of the body thin, the simile of *tuṭi iṭai* stands appropriate and pleasing.

Since the shape is broad at the top and the bottom and slender at the middle, can we bring in a grindstone for the simile? No, such a simile is not acceptable.

That is why this verse states that whether in the case of the simile or the thing which is compared to, they should be acceptable as well as comparable.

The commentator Pērāciriyar states that while the comparison of tresses to the feathers of a peacock is acceptable, its comparison with the feathers of a crow would jar.
