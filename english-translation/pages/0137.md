# Blossom 40 — The Tamiḻ Concept of Love · continued

> That comes in for acclaim  
> In Tamiḻ  
> Sweet as the strain of the lute  
> And the deep-sea pearl --  
> Is no thing of a lowly worth  
> Even to men of lowly birth.

***

Here is the *Tolkāppiyam* verse:

> Examined rightly,  
> The union of love between a man and a woman,  
> Which embraces the fivefold love behaviour mutually realized,  
> (Union, forbearance, separation, sulking and lamentation)  
> And which draws on the pleasures of love, life’s plenty and righteousness,  
> Shares the nature of the love of the *gandharvas* adept at lyre play  
> Which is among the eight forms of marriage vogue among the vedic folk.
>
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Kaḷaviyal*, verse 1)

Note: Tolkāppiyar had to make a reference to the vedic forms of marriage and contrast them with those of the Tamiḻ culture, for during his time, the vedic culture started taking roots on the Tamiḻ soil. The eight kinds of vedic marriage contrast with the three kinds treated in *Tolkāppiyam*, namely: mutual love (*aṉpiṉ aintiṇai*), one-sided love (*kaikkiḷai*) and abnormal sexual passion (*peruntiṇai*).

- Veḷḷaivāraṇar.
