# Blossom 29 — A Virtuous Woman’s Lamentation · continued

> To feast on the fragrant juice  
> From the temples of the elephant grand and stately;  
> Come the hour of the eve,  
> It seeks its craving appeased  
> By feeding on the scented jasmine opening up  
> On the honey-smelling tresses of women,  
> Who hold their loved ones in embrace,  
> So fast with their plump forearms  
> As to leave their men bruised by their bracelets;  
> The pain of hunger gone  
> The beetle goes oblivious  
> Taking off its mind for ever  
> From the blossoms of the pond  
> Which it feasted on ere in passion.

While bringing the blue water lily flowers for sale from a field filled with an abundance of water, the bees also accompany, swarming around the flowers. These bees happen to taste the juice of the rutting elephant on the way. The bees, indulging in drinking the fluid during the day resort to enjoying mullai flowers at night. Later, without even thinking about any other flower, they tend to forget their abode, which is a beautiful pond filled with water.

Since blue water lily flowers, bees, elephant’s juice, mullai flower and pond filled with flowers are there to see, this would seem like a verbal portrait of bees and flowers.

But in the context of the heroine’s separation from the hero, there are subtle parallels between the hero and the bees, prostitutes and the rutting elephant’s fluid, concubines and the mullai blossoms, and the pond and the home where the heroine suffers in loneliness.

There is no explicit comparison of such things by the heroine. It is the evocation of this spectacle in nature by which the heroine bares out her hurt.

And the following Tolkāppiyam verse provides the grammar of the Kali poem above:

> The suggestive simile is one  
> Where  
> The tenor comes revealing  
> As it draws implicitly on the vehicle described.
>
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Akattiṇai iyal*, verse 48).
