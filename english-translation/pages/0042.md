# Blossom 5 — Ethnic Unity · continued

Thus our Tamil consonants are divided into three classes. That is why I said, “The Tamilians are one race, while the Tamil language is of three races.”

After hearing this, a group of youth who were yet to be fully initiated into Tamil grammar stood up agitatedly and raised war-cries.

“What is this , sir? Wouldn’t our Tamil language’s fame get tarnished if her letters stand apart in three groups?”

An unperturbed Vaḷavaṉ came forward to allay their doubts in a balanced manner:

“Young men of valour! That you are apparently agitated with fears about the slight of the language makes me thrilled. But then, a five-footer like me would feel elated like a six-footer if you channelize your energy to oppose men smearing our Tamil language. You see, unlike the Tamil race, the Tamil language in her own characteristic style unites *vallinam, mellinam* and *iṭaiyiṉam* in itself and stands lofty.”

“How? How?” enquired the youth.

“Brothers! Sisters! In Tamil, /t/ is *vallinam*, /m/ is *mellinam* and /l/ *iṭaiyiṉam*. In this way, our classical Tamil language unites three diverse races of consonant letters to make a harmonious whole. But then, the practitioners of the Tamil language are split into various groups and stand apart. Do think about this. When *vallinam, mellinam* and *iṭaiyiṉam* blend together and make a lustrous entity called Tamil, is this correct on the part of the Tamilians to be divided into so many groups?”

The gathering dispersed and the youth took leave of each other after discussing among themselves, this nagging question: “When the word Tamil is formed by the blending of letters from three different classes, should the Tamil race stand divided?” There was only a deep sigh.

***

> /k/, /c/, /ṭ/, /t/, /p/ and /ṟ/ are called hard consonants.  
> /ṅ/, /ñ/, /ṇ/, /n/, /m/ and /ṉ/ are called soft consonants.  
> /y/, /r/, /l/, /v/, /ḻ/ and /ḷ/ are called medial consonants.
>
> (*Eḻuttatikāram : Nūl Marapu*, verses 19, 20 and 21)
