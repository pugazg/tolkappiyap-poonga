# Blossom 39 — Instability of Life and Things Worldly · continued

> *Pālai nilai*, the words of resentment by the wife,  
> Who prepares to enter into the funeral pyre of her husband,  
> At those that tend to weaken her resolve;  
> *Talaippeyalnilai*, the supreme act of the mother  
> That went seeking death on the battlefield  
> On hearing that her son had a retreat in war;  
> And *kāṭuvāḻttu*, the glorification of the graveyard  
> That perpetually impresses upon the people of this wide world  
> The impermanence of the lives of men  
> And its own [graveyard’s] permanence -  
> These ten substrands of worthy treatment including,  
> *Kāñci* theme ramifies into twenty sub-situations.
>
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Puṟattiṇai iyal*, verse 24)

***

> Be it the illumination of the little oil-lamp,  
> I have provided the expositions of all thematic situations  
> Included in *Akattiṇai* and *Puṟattiṇai*;  
> Although elucidations detailed of all these situations  
> I could not give in this study,  
> Could I afford to leave out *Kāñcittiṇai*?  
> Would not *kāñci* rebuke me albeit gently?  
> Don’t I know that honeyed heart?
>
> I am the one  
> Whose time of the day is split into atoms,  
> One atom of which is multiplied by a hundred  
> And is devoted exclusively to *kāñci*  
> And spent in all pleasure.
>
> The *kāñci* theme unmatched  
> Is to make a life of eternity  
> By being reminded of one’s instability of life  
> Bringing in the analogy of bubbles on water’s surface,  
> Providing an anchor to the mind vacillating  
> And standing on renown hill-like.
