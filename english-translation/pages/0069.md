# Blossom 16 — Female Son · continued

“She says the term female son (*peṇ makaṉ*) could be used.”

“How could it be? Female son? Are you referring to a eunuch?”

“No, no! Seems, it was in vogue to call the girls in pre-pubescent stage by the term female son. That is why Aṉṉam says ‘female son came’ (with feminine verbal suffix) could very well be used.”

“Okay, okay. There is no denying the fact that she has gone bonkers! She is fantasizing quite interestingly. Lunatic, lunatic! She is no doubt a lunatic!”

“Who is mad? You Taṅkam! Marakatam! It is not my own concept!”

A shouting Aṉṉam made her appearance with *Tolkāppiyam* in her hand. She opened the relevant page and said sarcastically, “Here, open your eyes properly and read this:

> The nominal terms *ellārum* and *ellīrum*  
> And the term *makaṉ* that follows *peṇ*  
> Are of the same kind too,  
> [They mark distinctions of gender within the human-class],  
> So do the learned say.

Again,

> The aberrant feminine gender designation *peṇmakaṉ*  
> Takes the verb concord  
> In the manner of a feminine gender denoting term.

Do you know what this verse in *Collatikāram* means? To put it in a nutshell:

The expression female son, which is a breach of gender, would take feminine gender—‘the girl came’(with the feminine verbal suffix).”

Aṉṉam elucidated further: “Terms like ‘everybody’. ‘all persons’ and female son are human class words. In the past, it was in vogue to call girls in pre-pubescent phase by the term female son.”

Taṅkam and Marakatam were completely astonished and listened to Aṉṉam without blinking their eyelids.

“Here is the *Tolkāppiyam* verse. Read it as well as its commentary first and decide as to whether Aṉṉam is really mad.” Thus thundered Aṉṉam and made her exit.
