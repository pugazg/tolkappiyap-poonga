# Blossom 61 — Without the Fox Getting Drenched...! · continued

Dear, if you are afraid of coming in broad daylight  
You can as well come lurking in the darkness of night.

Are you afraid of the wretched moon’s light?  
At dusk, once the sun vanishes you can plan your visit.

Obstacles may abound in thwarting your visit.  
But only you possess the medicine for my withering heart.

Even the flower I sport loses its fragrance in your absence.  
Have you not learnt the art of outwitting the local guards  
Who are active throughout the day?

The path you have to tread is full of stones and thorns  
I do know how difficult a path it is.

Maybe it is well  
That your journey is not taken keeping in mind the nature of the path.  
But it will be a pleasure for me  
If you were to come here to wipe the tears off my eyes.  
Many a time have we planned to meet,  
But on thinking we know it to be risky.  
And what remains is confusion.

Even our families have become apprehensive;  
As one alleviates one’s hunger without waiting for one’s turn,  
Rushing into marriage is the only way out.

Folding the palmyra letter,  
The hero gave a tender twist to the emissary’s ear.  
“I told you then itself that you can bathe the fox without getting it wet.  
Tell your friend I shall not fail my word.  
Let the month of *Thai* be born,  
A way out will have been born too,” he said.

The young woman responded,  
“A child would also be born in the tenth mouth.”  
She asked further,  
“What does bathing the fox without getting it wet mean?
