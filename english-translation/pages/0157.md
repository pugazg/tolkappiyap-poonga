# Blossom 47 — Wedded Love · continued

Look at the *Tolkāppiyam* reference:

> ... the land of four extended boundaries,  
> The land abounding in cool groves  
> And under the reign of three monarchs of benign note.
>
> (*Ceyyuḷiyal*, verse 75)

Just as *Karpiyal* section in *Tolkāppiyam* speaks of *karaṇam* “as taking the hand of the heroine in marriage as offered by those of a worthy line,” *Kaḷaviyal*, given the fact of elopement, refers to marriage as happening without the parental consent.

> The wedded course is  
> The hero, who comes from a worthy lineage,  
> Taking the hand in marriage  
> Of the heroine  
> As she is offered by those of a line worthy and compatible,  
> With the rites and ceremonies attendant thereon.
>
> Even as the heroine’s kin do not consent to their union  
> There does occasion wedding solemnized  
> With the two taking to elopement.
>
> The ceremonies and rites  
> Attendant on the three superior social classes  
> [kings, brahmins and traders]  
> Came to mark the marriage  
> Among those down the ladder in the course of time.  
> So has it been said.
>
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Karpiyal* , verses 1,2,3)

Note: What the commentator Iḷampūraṇar means when says that ‘the upper three’ comprises brahmins, kings and traders and the lower is the fourth caste, viz., Vēḷāḷar, is that in the ancient days marriage was common among all sections of the society and that it came to exclude the farming folks in the later days.

Impliedly, the days when marriage was common among all sections represent the period of antiquity to which caste division was unknown.

‘The exclusion of Vēḷāḷar’ refers to the division of society into castes when each caste came to be governed by its own codes of conduct. During this period of social segregation, the Vēḷāḷars refused to toe the line of vedic prescriptions.
