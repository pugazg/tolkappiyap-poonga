# Blossom 72 — Four Sources of Laughter · continued

One fine day, the vendor shouted and threatened the driver of a horse carriage as was his wont. “Do you know what I would do? I will break your carriage to pieces. Why do you stare? Do you know what will happen if I come there?” Just as the vendor was at his abusive best, the driver stopped the carriage and dared him to come near.

The vendor was dumbstruck. The reason being his inability to do so, as he was handicapped, without legs.

This evoked laughter from all quarters.

(Ridicule suits only such particular situations. It does not suit the disabled in general, neither should it do so.)

## Youth

(Since Pāṇṭiyaṉ Neṭuñceḻiyaṉ was young, he was mocked at by the other kings. An undaunted Neṭuñceḻiyaṉ rose up in arms against them. My poem below celebrates the exploits of this youth: )

The jingling anklet on the leg of this tender youth  
Was replaced by a string of bells marking heroism.

The plaited lock was cut off.  
He was a boy-king holding a dazzling sword.

He took off the bangles from his tender hands  
And sported the bow on his shoulder.  
He was still an adolescent wearing amulet.

Therefore, holding him too young,  
The two crowned monarchs, Cēra and Cōḻa.  
Together with the five mighty *vēḷir* chieftains challenged him for a war.

The famous army of the five chieftains paired with  
The kings holding the banners sporting arrow and tiger,  
The young Pāṇṭiyaṉ was never perturbed  
At the event of crossing swords with the alliance of the seven.

Looking at the message from the enemy,  
“Better be chastised, otherwise you may have to part with very life,”  
He laughed aloud and roared:  
“It is not in our tradition to hide from war.
