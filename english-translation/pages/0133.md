# Blossom 39 — Instability of Life and Things Worldly · continued

> *Kāñci* theme is that  
> What tells folks  
> To preserve honour  
> Be it at the cost of one’s head.
>
> *Kāñci* theme tells men  
> To perform deeds virtuous on the earth  
> Leaving not a bit of it incomplete  
> With the realization that  
> ‘He that lived yesterday  
> Does exist not today’  
> Even when there remains the threat  
> Of the power of Death around.
>
> Even if you stand uncared for,  
> Spirits too will come to your aid  
> Hailing your efforts large  
> To make the Tamils flourish.
>
> *Kāñci* is routing the wily foe  
> In the manner of the Pāṇṭiya king  
> Who swore: “Let me not be the theme of Māṅkuṭi Marutaṉ’s song  
> If I don’t vanquish the foes and return!
>
> *Kāñci* is  
> That act of the woman  
> Who bathes with her tears  
> Her dead husband wounded in war,  
> Prohibits others from touching her  
> And stabs herself  
> With the sword that killed her husband.
>
> The unmatched *kāñci* is that  
> Where a father and mother in the evening of their life  
> See the suitor for their daughter driven away  
> With wounds on his back  
> Who came waging a war for her hand.
