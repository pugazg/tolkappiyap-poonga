# Blossom 23 — Women and Palm Leaves · continued

“Do you know the lifestyle of people of the yesteryears, their beliefs and practices? Whatever these beliefs - everything is represented without any omission in literature. Do you know something, Mukil? The Tolkāppiyar, who speaks about prohibition of women from undertaking voyages, has also chronicled the privilege conferred on women in those days.”

“What sort of privilege?”

“In order to get his beloved by any means, the hero used to ride on a horse of palmyra stems in the ancient days.”

“How cruel! The practice of riding on palmyra horses involved men sitting over bundles of palmyra leaves, going down the street of the town of the heroine and persuading the elders of the town to get him the heroine. The dried leaves of the palmyra would cause bruises all over his body.

In order to save women from experiencing such an excruciating pain, women were prevented from undergoing this process in those days!”

“To realize our love we men underwent *maṭalēṟutal*; but you women--to achieve the objective of your love...”

“We need not undergo the practice of *maṭalēṟutal*; instead a mere *maṭal* (letter) would do the trick.”

“Is that so? Is that so easy? Shall I give it a try?”

“I am sure you mean our marriage invitation!” a blushing Mullaikkoṭi started leaving, taking the books back and holding them tight to her bosom.

“Wish I had been those books!” Though Mukilaṉ didn’t exactly utter these words, his mischievous glance looked like saying so.

***

> No strand of Akam love behaviour  
> Does enjoin on the heroine  
> To ride on the horse of palmyra stems  
> As it goes contrary to the norms of feminine propriety.  
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Akattiṇai iyal*, verse 35).
