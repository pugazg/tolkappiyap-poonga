# Preface

*Tolkāppiyam* is the earliest extant work in Tamil. That it does not begin with an invocation to God is one of its unique characteristics. It has a prologue heralding the text composed by Paṉampāraṇār, Tolkāppiyar's contemporary and learning-mate. A look at this prologue would make us hold our heads high, involuntarily, outlining as it does the boundaries of the ancient Tamil country, in which *Tolkāppiyam*, the momentous grammar of the glorious Tamil language, was composed:

> In the virtuous Tamil- speaking land  
> That extends from  
> Vēṅkaṭam in the north  
> And Kumari in the south,  
> He has treated units of sound and word  
> And semantic content as well,  
> Rooted as he remained  
> In traditions of common speech and literary idiom;  
> Conversant he was with his predecessors' works  
> That were in vogue and use on the soil  
> Which marked the cultivated idiom of the Tamil language;  
> He examined them in order  
> And has made a well-wrought work;  
> He presented this classic  
> In the assembly of Nilantarutiruvin Pāṇṭiyaṉ  
> Where at the head was Ataṅkōṭṭācāṉ
