# Blossom 35 — That Golden Era · continued

Both the kings at the start of the war place their swords under the decorated canopy at an auspicious hour and take them in procession.

The soldiers defending the fort from inside and the ones attacking it from outside wage a fierce war against each other, either standing on or climbing over the ladders made of wooden planks which are placed on the fort walls.

The spectacle of the soldiers inside and outside the fort fighting each other and retreating resembles the joining and moving away of the parasitic plant in the moat around the fort wall due to the strong winds or the vibration caused by the deed of war.

The battle extends to many parts of the town too.

Not completely relying upon the soldiers from both the sides and in order to demonstrate the steely resolve of their hearts, the warring kings fight on the wall of the fort themselves, without ever worrying about their lives.

After all, the garland of victory is meant for only one of them! Before the defeat could stare large on his face, a king saves his dignity by embracing death.

The purificatory ceremonies are performed for the sword of the victor king, as it is deemed to be responsible for his triumph.

It was the ethos of those days to hold aloft the white canopy over the sword and thereby pay obeisance to the sword, which sealed victory in the war by being the deity in the santorum and the procession deity. Yes, it was the era which recognized and honoured valour. In spite of being an era of wars, it certainly was the golden epoch hailing the greatness of the Tamiḻ race, in that the victor king was being honoured with coronation in the name of the deceased king. Is not this a mark of magnanimity of our ancestors? Look at the *Tolkāppiyam* verse:

> *Uḻiñai* marks twelve other substrands too:  
> Sending of the royal parasol and sword at an auspicious hour  
> Preceding the siege of the foe intended;  
> The raging fight of the soldiers  
> As they stand on ladders fitted to the fort;  
> The fierce, resolute battle  
> Intended to end the siege outside the fortress;  
> The mighty defence offered by the besieged within;  
> Breach of the defence by the besieging men;  
> Action in the moat of the fortress;  
> Carrying the battle thereon
