# Blossom 37 — The Concept of Victory · continued

According to Tolkāppiyar, Brahmins had six duties - to chant mantras , make others chant mantras , conduct homams, make others conduct homams, to give and to accept gifts. The king’s five cardinal duties were - to chant mantras, to conduct homams, to give gifts, to offer army’s support to allies, and to protect the subjects. The traders and peasants had six duties - to chant mantras, to conduct homams, to give gifts, to cultivate, to trade and to maintain the livestock. To calculate and monitor the events of the three times, namely, the past, the present, and the future, was the duty of persons endowed with wisdom. The recluse had the duties of upholding eight types of discipline. While being aware of these duties, the army leaders’ duty was to accomplish victory, not only through the might of sword or arms, but also through the means of these duties. Such a victory was supposed to be more enduring.

Though many of these duties underlined could uplift one’s stature of life, the two duties of conducting homams, and making others follow the practice of conducting homams provided opportunities for the Brahmins and such people to resort to discrimination which was completely alien to the Tamil land.

Vēṭal means to conduct homams, while vēṭpittal is to make others conduct homams.

The practice of offering sacrifice and conducting homams crept into the Tamil land through the wiles of the Brahmins.

It took roots and branched out among the Tamils thanks to the cleverness of the brahmins. Victory could be amassed through bravery which required waging wars and perseverance. Real victory in life and happiness could be achieved by getting involved in various virtuous deeds, even cutting across national boundaries. What then was the necessity for these unavailing practices of conducting homams and offering sacrifices? There would have been reservations and protests. But would the priestly class allow this to be given up?

If the subtle manipulation combined with versatile abilities worked, it was natural that even an emperor would be hypnotized in the meandering smoke of camphor.

Therefore, conducting homams and the practice of making others conduct homams started slowly but gained momentum of a forest flood and breached the banks.

Those who wielded arms like spear, sword, lance and trident and rode on horses and those who sang of the power of love and the breathlike passion for the motherland had to stoop before the fire pit of the homam, prostrate and remain in a hunched posture to drink the urine of cow, in keeping with the practice of conducting homams.
