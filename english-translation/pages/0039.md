# Blossom 4 — Medicine, Tablet · continued

“Ay, the pain hasn’t subsided in one tablet; the midwife has prescribed two”, concluded the two lurking men and decided to abandon their nefarious mission in order to leave the pregnant woman alone.

“Look here, girl! The five sounds *a, i, u, e* and *o* take one māttirai each, while the seven sounds *ā, ī, ū, ē, ai, ō* and *au* are elongated taking two māttirai each. In the same manner, consonant sounds take half a māttirai each.”

“Madam! You have clearly explained the classes of letters. But I have a doubt.”

“Okay girl! What is your doubt?”

“You spoke about one māttirai, two māttirai and half a māttirai. What are they?”

“Well, the winking of eyes or the snapping of fingers forms one unit of time called māttirai. Do you understand it now?”

“I understand, madam. The units of sound (māttirai) in Tolkāppiyam have evoked sympathy among the thieves as they mistook them for the grumbling of a pregnant woman and left the place.”

“Is it so? How do you know?”

“Madam, your vision is a bit low; but I saw them in the balcony and overheard their conversation.”

“Look, girl! Tamil grammar has come to our rescue. Medicine could be consumed in tablet form but Tolkāppiyar has described the duration of sounds in terms of māttirai.

Let me elucidate further:

> The five among the thirty —  
> /a/, /i/, /u/, /e/ and /o/ —  
> Are known to be short vowels  
> Articulated by single phonic measures (māttirai)  
> So do the learned hold.
>
> (*Eḻuttatikāram, Nūl Marapu*, verse 3)

> The seven among the thirty —  
> /ā/, /ī/, /ū/, /ē/, /ai/ and /au/ —  
> Are known to be long vowels  
> Taking two such phonic measures,  
> Thus has the authority ruled.
>
> (*Eḻuttatikāram, Nūl Marapu*, verse 4)
