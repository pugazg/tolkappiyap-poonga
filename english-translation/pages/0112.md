# Blossom 34 — Outsmarting the Smartest

Even while ruling over the entire land, people would go for reigning supreme over the water. After possessing the land and the water, they would like to venture for the sky and capture that also. The bees swarming around over the honeycomb would fly up and above and think the land and the sky are well within their reach. It is no wonder then that a chieftain no longer remains content with being under the overlordship of a king. Further, the coveted fame of possessor of many a fortress also gets satiating. When the land comes, he retorts, “You alone? Let the sea also assemble here to obey my orders!’ If that improbable also happens, then he desires for the eastern sky of the morning and of the western sky of the dusk to extend a red carpet welcome to him!

*Tolkāppiyam*’s *vañcittiṇai* focuses upon such persons of overvaulting ambitions. Further, to extend the boundaries of their kingdoms, they desire large tracts of land and consequently invade the neighbouring countries. It then also happens that the invaded stand up in prowess and humble the aggressor.

> It (*veṭci*) is  
> The vanquishing of a king  
> Marked by uncontained hunger for other’s soil,  
> By another king  
> Through a frightful display of prowess.
>
> (*Poruḷatikāram : Puṟattiṇai iyal*, verse 7)
