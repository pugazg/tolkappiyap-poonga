# Blossom 30 — The Heroine of the Agricultural Tract

There is the fourfold division of land, along with a fifth, viz., the arid tract, a forced addition though it seems. Each type of land has a specified presiding deity right from the era of Tolkāppiyam. The agricultural land’s presiding deity ,Vēntaṉ, that was in vogue from the days of Tolkāppiyar was incidentally transformed into Intiraṉ after a few hundred years, owing to the onslaught of other cultures.

Be that as it may, the kaikkiḷai (unrequited love) theme of Akam love, though unknown today for its grammatical significance, makes its presence felt in day-to-day life. That has been picturised as a dramatic scene here:

The inhabitants of the agricultural tract celebrate the festival of Intiraṉ. Decked in lotus and water lily flowers, people gather under the shade of the groves of kāñci, marutam and vañci trees, play marutam melody on their marutam lutes, take bath in the freshets and enjoy themselves. A drama stage comes into existence and the Cakuntalam play is enacted.
