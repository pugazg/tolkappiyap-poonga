# Blossom 86 — Know Your Simile

> Four are the grounds for comparison  
> Between the tenor and the vehicle:  
> The deed done, the effect thereof  
> The form and the complexion.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Uvamaiyiyal*, verse 1)

A simile compares something with something else using the words like or as, for example,

a row of teeth like a string of pearls

Here ‘a row of teeth’ is the subject of comparison, and ‘a string of pearls’ is the simile or the standard of comparison.

In Sanskrit, the subject of comparison is called *uvamēyam* and the standard of comparison is known as *upamānam*. These terms are frequently used in the teaching of similes.

Comparisons are made between things on the basis of four common characteristics, viz., complexion, form, deed and effect. The comparison markers in Tamil are *aṉṉa, āṅka, pōla*, etc. (meaning like or as)

e.g. *mayilaṉṉa cāyal* - peacock’s like grace  
*māṉ pōlat tuḷḷiṉāl* - she hopped like a deer.
