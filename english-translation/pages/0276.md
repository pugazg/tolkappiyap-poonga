# Blossom 99 — The Lamp on the Hill · continued

Such dos and don’ts are in order in a book too, which the following verse lists:

> A study sharp-eyed  
> Points to defects that stain a composition:  
> Tautology, inconsistency, understatement,  
> Overstatement, verbiage, obscurity,  
> Cacophony, contemptible things in unworthy diction,  
> Persisting in one’s own pet ideas,  
> Incomprehensibility of varied sorts  
> And failings that issue from these.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Marapiyal*, verse 109)

Tautology is saying the same thing again which is unnecessary. Inconsistency is saying things which are contradictory to one another. Understatement refers to making something seem less important or less impressive than it really is. Overstatement is its opposite. Verbiage is the use of too many words or of more difficult words than needed to express an idea. Obscurity is being difficult to understand. Cacophony is a mixture of loud unpleasant sounds. Such of these defects will not add to the worth of a book.

A book free from these shortcomings will be like the bright lamp on a hillock enlightening minds. Now what are the features of excellence of a composition?

> Brevity and perspicuousness,  
> Pleasing, worthy diction,  
> Pleasing sounds,  
> Depth of thought and ideas,  
> Right organization,  
> Acceptability in a large measure,  
> Sublimity  
> And coherence.

(*Naṉṉūl*)
