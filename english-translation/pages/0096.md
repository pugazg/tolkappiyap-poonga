# Blossom 28 — The Call of Duty and the Predicament of the Hero

There might be many reasons for men to leave women. Separation means unbearable anxiety for women. The man takes much pains to console his woman and take leave of her. Separation is inevitable for men. They must go to train in warfare or to acquire knowledge for enriching their minds. If trained in these skills, separation happens on account of protection of the state leaving behind the object of one’s love.

The woman doesn’t take the parting for good, though her loneliness is rather short. Since both of them live so intimately as not to let a thread to pass through their embrace, she regards each day of separation as a sword chopping her heart.

Even though he parts only to learn Tamiḻ literature and grammar and receive training in warfare after explaining the benefits of his activities to the home and the country, she bids adieu only after shedding copious tears, waterfall like.

If the hero happens to be a king bound by the responsibilities of the state such as aiding a friendly ruler, or if he goes on an ambassadorial mission, he has to be valorous or wise as the occasion demands.
