# Blossom 87 — The Excellence of the Simile

The excellence of the simile adds to the worth of the thing compared.

> As we examine them,  
> The vehicles of comparison  
> Do stand loftier than the tenors.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Uvamaiyiyal*, verse 3)

When we say, ‘the face is as beautiful as a red lotus’, the thing compared is the face. The standard of comparison, the red lotus, gives here an added significance to the face.

The simile should be worthier than the thing compared.

He stood like a lion.

In this simile, we don’t research as to whether the lion or the man is greater. Such an argument will only undermine the importance of the simile. However, in this case, the emphasis is on the strength and majesty of the lion. Thus the thing taken for comparison gains pre-eminence over the thing compared.

The simile becomes efficient when an attribute like “pouncing tiger” is given to a character like Paṇṭāraka Vaṉṉiyaṉ by way of comparative description.

On the other hand, in the expression ‘Shivaji mountain mouse like,’ the significance of the simile goes down as the person compared is of a high stature.

We have to consider this rather an exception.
