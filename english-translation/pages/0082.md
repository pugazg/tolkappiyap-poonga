# Blossom 22 — Reward for Objection · continued

“O my goodness! You don’t know what sulking means! Among the verses in *Iṉpattuppāl* section of *Tirukkuṟaḷ*, those under “sulking” are the best. The man and woman have a feigned quarrel against each other, which ultimately gets resolved leading to ecstasy.”

“In the first place, why should they sulk and then settle down to reconciliation?”

“Sulking is entering into a quarrel with the sole aim of getting reconciled.”

“Okay then, what are you waiting for? Start the quarrel.”

“I am going to break a piece of news, most disliked by you.”

“What is it?”

“The dawn tomorrow would see me sailing to Rome.”

“Oh, no. Don’t tell me you are off to a foreign land once again.”

“Yes, we need to carry the pearls of Tamil Nāṭu for sale in Rome.”

“Alas! Was I born to be tossed like a catamaran by the ebb and tide of the sea waves? No, I am determined to stop your voyage.”

“No obstacles please! Let there be no embers in your eyes! If I sell pearls overseas, gold coins would pile like a flood unshuttered. So dear lady, let me take leave of you.”

“When I, like the lamp of the house, wait for you here with my fingers crossed, you tear apart the tumultuous sea to go for trading. I would have breathed my last by the time you returned.”

“Come on! Look here! You are soon going to break into pearls of laughter, which certainly doesn’t grow in the shell. Oh chaste Tamil, which abide in my heart! You are going to accompany me to Rome. Is that okay?”

After hearing this sweet news she planted some mighty kisses and embraced her lord.

Everybody in the classroom appreciated Mukilaṉ with a thunderous applause.

The Tamil professor said, “What students? Shall we confer the first prize on Mukilaṉ? But one thing. This script doesn’t completely belong to Mukilaṉ.

It reads like part of a dialogue in the play *Pūmpukār*!”

Student Mullaikkoṭi intervened: “Professor sir! Friend Mukilaṉ’s work doesn’t conform to the norms of ancient Tamil literature and culture. Nor is it completely like the dialogue in *Pūmpukār*.”

“What is your objection? If you could reflect what is in my mind, you will get the prize.”
