# Blossom 6 — Production of Speech Sounds - How Admirable! · continued

A smiling Paṉampāraṉār replied, “Quite correct. In language, sounds appeared first and the corresponding letters followed only later.

Description of the speech organs for the production of these individual sounds has a scientific basis for sure. This holds true while pronouncing the Tamil words *vā* and *pō*. The word *vā* is pronounced as the upper teeth ridge comes into contact with the folded lower lip, while the word *pō* is pronounced with the airstream forced out between the lower and upper lips closing and opening. This is what Tolkāppiyar refers to as airstream originating right from the navel, getting upwardly mobile to reach the head, neck and chest, and passing through teeth, lips, tongue, nose and hard palate. On the whole, airstream passes through eight human organs to produce relevant sounds. Among them, the twelve vowel sounds come from the pharynx: *a* and *ā* are articulated by opening the mouth. The five vowel sounds *u, ū, o, ō,* and *au* are said with lips rounded. The tongue touches the lower palate to produce *k* and *ṅ*. Some people don’t differentiate between *l* and *ḷ* and pronounce *kulam*, wrongly as *kuḷam*, and *kaḷam* as *kalam* which strike a jarring note. That is why Tolkāppiyar tells us to pronounce *l* with the tongue touching the hard palate and *ḷ* with the tongue stroking the palate. Such systematic articulation through organs of speech either touching each other or gently stroking each other sounds sweet to the ear. If wrongly pronounced, as *kol* for *koḷ* or *moli* for *moḻi*, it will feel like the strike of thunder, won’t it?”

Paṉampāraṉār’s explanation evoked a vociferous laugh among the poets.

One poet intervened, “Respected sir, all the linguists have formulated such detailed phonetic explanations for their languages, especially the Sanskrit scholars, which Tolkāppiyar too refers to in the lines:

> The phonic measure of each distinctive sound (māttirai)  
> Even as it is so shaped within  
> Is examined and shown in terms unimpeachable  
> In the scriptured treatises of the brahmins (Antaṇar).”

On hearing this, Paṉampāraṉār got agitated and retorted, “No sir, no! It is wrong. Antaṇar does not refer to the Aryans; nor *maṟai* points to their vedas. Among the Tamilians, the intellectuals are referred to as Antaṇar while their great works are held as works of scripture. Therefore, kindly don’t give credit to somebody else for the work of our scholars. It is wrong, a sin even, to forget our unique achievements ourselves, and to stand by others who gloss over such achievements.”

Thus saying, Paṉampāraṉār left the hall leaving an atmosphere of silence behind.
