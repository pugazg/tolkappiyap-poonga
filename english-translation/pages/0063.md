# Blossom 14 — My Son? · continued

“If it is a self-respect marriage, then why are you sticking to auspicious date and stars?”

“Don’t you know? On certain occasions, vedic rituals would be insisted upon by bridegroom’s side, whereas, at times, bride’s side would take turns to sticking to them. Here, for the sake of the bride’s family - the astrological details. And in accordance with our wish, self-respect marriage.”

The manager smiled and scrutinized the marriage invitation given to him by Kaliyaperumāḷ for printing. “Is everything okay? Do you know as to why I am asking this? You possess proficiency in Tamil with a knack of rectifying even minor mistakes! That is why we prefer this press!”

“Everything is correct except for one mistake!”

“What is it?”

“Not exactly a big mistake. While writing bridegroom’s name, you have mentioned it as ‘my son’ (*eṉatu makaṉ*).”

“Yes, the bridegroom is my son...”

“Sir, that is exactly the mistake.”

“What are you saying, Meyyappaṉ? He is my son, otherwise, who else is he?”

“That is not correct.”

“You give back that paper! What blabbering is this?”

Kaliyaperumāḷ snatched the paper from the hands of Meyyappaṉ. Just then, Meyyappaṉ caught hold of his hand and pleaded:

“My apologies. You only commended my Tamil proficiency, then how come you get agitated?”

“Okay; What do you want to say? Speak out!”

“The sixth case marker *atu* in *eṉatu makaṉ* is wrong. We must use the fourth case marker *ku* and write *eṉakku makaṉ*.”

“I hardly know anything about case markers and distinctions of human and non-human classes. Because you say this, it must be correct.”

“I don’t say that! *Tolkāppiyam* states so!”

“Whosoever might say so, there is no denying the fact that he is my son! Kindly finish the invitation printing work soon. And here is your advance amount. Now, let me take your leave.”
