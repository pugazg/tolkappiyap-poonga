# Blossom 91 — Metaphor or Simile? · continued

Here the breast and the face are similes and the bud and the flower the subjects. Because the breast and the face are shown to be loftier, they are the standards of comparison.

Turned into metaphors, they are ‘the bud of the breast’ and ‘the lotus of the face’. Given the fact that the simile stands on a higher pedestal in relation to the subject, there is no obscurity with regard to the subject and the simile. Pērāciriyar’s explanation runs along these lines.

Later rhetoricians term ‘the lotus of the face’ a metaphor and classify it as a separate figure of speech. Veḷḷaivāraṇar, basing himself on the commentaries of Iḷampūraṇar and Pērāciriyar, looks at it as a variety of the simile.
