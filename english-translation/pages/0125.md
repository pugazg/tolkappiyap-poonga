# Blossom 37 — The Concept of Victory · continued

Even that great Cēraṉ Ceṅkuṭṭuvaṉ of *Cilappatikāram* who successfully led the Himalayan campaign and brought the stone for carving the statue of Kaṇṇaki on the heads of the northern kings Kaṉakaṉ and Vicayaṉ had to fall in line.

That renowned warrior was beaten squarely by the vedic rites and rituals.

The invasion followed by the bringing of stone for the Kaṇṇaki statue was over. Kaṉaka-Vicayaṉ were also imprisoned. At that juncture, a person of the Aryan priestly class, Māṭala Maṟaiyōṉ, met the king and offered advice, which bore fruit. The kings of northern origin were set free. In keeping with the advice of the priest, the king made preparations for a *homam*. That work was entrusted to the army chief Villavaṉkōtai:

> You, master of wisdom, listen to my words:  
> Vedic sacrifices prescribed for kings  
> You need to perform with priestly cooperation:  
> This will surely lead you to salvation,  
> That would gladden heavenly beings.  
> If you postpone it for tomorrow, who knows  
> For certain his last day? Supppose....”

> Māṭalaṉ prayed for long life to the king,  
> His consort, and the brave king kneeling  
> Before him; he did sow thoughts of higher being  
> In the mind of Kuṭṭuvaṉ, the Cēra king.  
> The sowing resulted in abundant harvest;  
> The valiant king, eager to enjoy the best,  
> Engaged priests, who had studied the vedas,  
> And had all doubts cleared by their *gurus*,  
> To perform the sacrificial rites to conform  
> To the wise Māṭalaṉ’s prescription.

> He released the Aryan kings from prison,  
> And lodged them in Velāvikkō mansion  
> Outside the city walls, and surrounded  
> By shady groves; he announced their freedom  
> To go home, after the sacrifices were concluded.  
> He instructed Villavaṉkōtai to show due courtesy to them.
