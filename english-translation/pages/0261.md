# Blossom 94 — It Was in Vogue

The comic script that follows explains that many a word found in the dictionary is not in use today, and a few of them have completely disappeared. Look at the verse below:

> Mōttai, takar, uṭal and appar  
> Designate the male sheep.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Marapiyal*, verse 48)

In Aṇṇā’s play *Nīti Tēvaṉ Mayakkam*, Rāvaṇaṉ files a case against Kampar. He takes umbrage at Kampar describing him as “a merciless demon” in his version of Rāmāyaṇa and he himself argues his case before the judge.

“I didn’t even touch Cīta, and safeguarded her in the garden of Ashoka. Juxtapose my chivalry with Intira’s who raped Akalya, Cantiraṉ who raped his teacher’s wife, Lakcumaṇaṉ who chopped off the nose and breast of Cūrpaṇakai, Lord Civa who misbehaved with the wives of the sages in Tārukāvaṇam and Rāma, who assailed Vāli hidden from a vantage point. How could I be termed merciless?” This is Rāvaṇaṉ’s defence.

The judge, out of his inability to pass a judgement against the gods, faints.

In the same manner, here comes another piece of imagination. Appar, Campantar, Cuntarar and Māṇikkavācakar were the four renowned Caiva devotees. They were also great poets in Tamil.

Among them, Tirunāvukkaracar who was known as Appar brings a case before the judge. His contention is that his name included under non-human beings (akṟiṇai) in Marapiyal of Tolkāppiyam must be deleted.
