# Blossom 46 — Two Interpretations : An Explanation · continued

Kaṇṇaki lost her husband and became a widow.  
The king hurled at her the charge of being a thief’s wife.

The vedic mantras recited by the brahmin priest  
And the propitious time of wedding fixed on the planetary motion  
Didn’t protect the conjugal harmony of the couple.  
The epic *Cilappatikāram* is witness to this fact.

The matchless *Tolkāppiyam*,  
That is the treasure trove of the Tamils  
And a fine flower-garden of language and literature,  
Preceded *Cilappatikāram* by a long span of time.

The culture of the Tamils  
Didn’t see complete degeneration  
In the early days of the Aryan advent.

The inroads of the alien influence nevertheless  
Were marked here and there.  
Why should it be denied?  
Tolkāppiyar himself makes pointed references to it.

In the clandestine love career,  
That precedes the wedded life of the lovers,  
The hero does not eschew rendezvous with the heroine  
On account of the inhospitable path of the wilderness,  
The intense vigil of the heroine’s kin,  
The perils due to the wild animals,  
Or the thought of the long travel to make.

And yet  
Commentator Nacciṉārkkiṉiyar presumes that  
The rendezvous used to be deferred  
For reasons of time and day inauspicious (*ōrai*).

Another commentator Iḷampūraṇar too  
Almost concurs in this regard with the former.

On the other hand,  
Veḷḷaivāraṇar’s *Tolkāppiyam* study  
Looks at the word *ōrai*
