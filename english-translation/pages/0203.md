# Blossom 66 — Greatness and Humility · continued

A passage in *Kamparāmāyaṇam* portrays Rāvaṇaṉ’s sense of honour and valour with awe-struck admiration. Before pointing it out, let us look at the pride of place given to the sulks between lovers in *Tolkāppiyam Poruḷiyal*.

*Tolkāppiyam* shows that however proud and manly a person might be, in the event of the feigned quarrel between husband and wife, the husband would have to stoop to pacify his wife.

> The dominance of the wife  
> And the suppliant postures of the husband  
> Are seen to mark  
> The moments of tiffs of passion between the two.

Bearing this in mind, let us take a look at Rāvaṇa’s speech demonstrating his self-esteem.

The wisemen, ripe counsellors and the learned approach Rāvaṇaṉ at his court to counsel him, “Defeat is imminent! Therefore, it is better to surrender than inviting destruction.” On listening to this, Rāvaṇaṉ declares:

Even at the time of sulks in marital life, it is not my wont to yield.

Surrender is unknown to Rāvaṇaṉ. “ I will not give in even in sulks with my wife in the bed-chamber, will I?

In *Teṉṉavaṉ Kātai* authored by me, years ago, I noted:

“It was only in the bed-chamber before his wife where the music of Rāvaṇaṉ’s harp faced defeat.”

My opinion was prompted by these lines from *Tolkāppiyam*.

> The dominance of the wife  
> And the suppliant postures of the husband  
> Are seen to mark  
> The moments of tiffs of passion between the two.

(*Poruḷatikāram : Poruḷiyal*, verse 33)
