# Blossom 29 — A Virtuous Woman’s Lamentation

*Tolkāppiyam* unfolds the rose of a poetic device called ‘implied simile’. The reason why it is called a rose is that it is a rose among thorns and is a literature of a grammatical concept.

To draw parallels between two things is known as a simile. The implied simile suggests things rather than explicitly compares them.

A *Marutakkali* poem provides a fine illustration of this mode of simile where it looks like providing a relish of a spectacle in nature that forms part of a village:

> O the lord of the virtuous land with fountains abounding,  
> Where  
> The beetle drones about the blue water-lily,  
> Which the vendors plucked  
> From the fields of copious waters  
> And are carrying to town;  
> Once in town,  
> The beetle moves off
