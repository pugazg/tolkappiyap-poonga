## Deprivation/loss (iḷavu)

India’s first Prime Minister, Nehru, who ruled for 17 long years, was hailed by all sections of the society - right from the common men to the elite. Here is a poem composed in *the form of the grief of the rose* which he sported on his coat on the death of Nehru, the light of Asia and the white dove of peace.

The nobel one! The man of principles!  
Where is that Death which robbed us of you?

Today, you took me and put me near your heart.  
*But you have left me* and gone far away. Is it fair?

You lent honey to my petals.  
Don’t you remember as to how I reciprocated that?

Has your heart turned into a stone,  
Depriving me of my joy overflowing?

On hearing the dirge, the other flowers approached the rose.  
Finding it in the flood of tears and is about to faint  
Jasmine went close and asked:  
“What Rose, why do you keep crying so long?  
A good person lived for the nation to benefit from.  
A munificent person lived to *embrace us all,*  
Could we ever get him back? *Stop your wailing.*  
The queen of flowers! Do smile.”
