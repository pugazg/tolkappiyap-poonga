# Blossom 63 — Modesty Dear as Life

‘To fear what ought to be feared is given to the wise’ (*Tirukkuṟaḷ*)

A dictionary compiled by a team of scholars records ‘humility’ and ‘reserve’ as the meanings of *accam* (fear), and *nāṇam* (modesty) means fear, humility, regard, shyness, etc.

In case our action brings with it disrepute, then it is necessary that we should fear it. Throwing fear overboard under such circumstances is never acceptable. When loss of honour is imminent, fear one should.

Likewise, modesty (feminine reserve) does not mean women lowering the gaze at the sight of men.

*Tolkāppiyam* prescribes that in any explanation of virtue, wealth and happiness, modesty has a role to play. Since the virtue of modesty is dear to great men as if it is their very life, it should never be lost sight of.

Such great men embody inner and outer realities exemplifying the ideal that the world is what such men make of. They are not the people who merely preach munificence, virtue and bliss, but hold modesty to be a supreme virtue.

The modesty discussed here is not the one that occurs between a lover and the beloved. It is about knowing under what conditions the practice of modesty exalts that very virtue, and organize our lives accordingly.
