# Blossom 17 — Finished Cooking; I Have Come · continued

She didn’t relish his loud reminders.

The *Tirukkuṟaḷ* verse on hospitality,

> The *anicham* flower withers away when smelt;  
> The guests would turn sullen when looked at with a frown.

kept ringing as a caution in her heart. Just as she was rushing through with cooking, she heard her husband’s harsh query, “At what time would you finish cooking? At what time would it be served? At what time would we be eating it?”

The lady wearing a smile on her face placed the plantain leaves on the floor and said, “Let me state the present time and future time in past tense -- here, I finished cooking. I am right here for serving. You three could come and start eating.” One of the friends was all praise for her:

“Looks the mistress of the house has a sound mastery over grammar also, as she reigns like a queen!”

“Well, I am no queen - I am an ordinary woman discharging my household duties and of course with some knowledge of literature. That is all.”

Her proud and loving husband enquired, “Isn’t this *Tolkāppiyam*? You can as well recite the entire verse.”

“Let the feast be over, then recitation can follow.”

After finishing the feast, she didn’t forget to serve the delicacy of *Tolkāppiyam*:

> To attribute the action of a verb to the past  
> While it bears on the present and future  
> Is to suggest exigency,  
> So do the literate hold.
>
> (*Collatikāram : Viṉaiyiyal*, verse 44)

Note: An event that links the present and the future times can be expressed in the form of past tense.
