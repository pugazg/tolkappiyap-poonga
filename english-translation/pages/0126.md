# Blossom 37 — The Concept of Victory · continued

In his elucidation of *Tolkāppiyam*, Nāvalar Cōmacuntara Pāratiyār categorically rejects the presence of caste-based classification of *Akattiṇai iyal* and *Puṟattiṇai iyal*.

The great scholar Veḷḷaivāraṇar subscribes to this view in his *Tolkāppiyam* studies and says: “The four types of caste observed in books of northern origin were unheard of among the ancient Tamiḻ people.” This was his categorical averment. He adds, “The practice of classifying people on the basis of caste was never prevalent in the ancient Tamiḻ country. The categorization was only on the basis of the geographical regions they inhabited.” Though these two learned academics claim so, it cannot be denied that the custom of classifying people on the basis of caste sneaked in either overtly or covertly and got established firmly.

In came castes to begin *homams* and sacrifices heralding the advent of the Brahmins in *vākaittiṇai* itself.

If one were to put it with a tinge of sadness, it would be rather more appropriate to describe *vākaittiṇai* as the ‘*tiṇai* of the Aryan advent’.
