# Blossom 11 — Grammatical Renunciation · continued

“It is a long time since some monks have forgotten and abandoned the norms of ascetic life. Well then, junior monk! Pray, which grammatical rule have I broken?”

“You used the plural verb while referring to one nocturnal chic, and in the same vein you referred to the single pontiff in the room using a plural suffix. According to your statement, how many pontiffs are there in the room? How many nocturnal chics? One pontiff and many nocturnal chics? Or, one nocturnal chic and many pontiffs?”

“Junior pontiff, don’t be confused! My question was based on a sound grammatical principle. The plural suffix is used for referring to a single man or woman. Singulars being referred to by plural suffixes are used as honorifics. It is indeed based on established grammatical rules which Tolkāppiyar calls admissible deviations.”

“Is that so!” exclaimed the junior monk with a gaping mouth, just as the door of the room opened. The colourful damsel released from the bedroom staggered her way out through the backdoor of the mutt. The teacher caught hold of the junior monk and recited the relevant verse from *Tolkāppiyam*:

> Plural suffix to a noun denoting human species singular  
> And plural suffix to a noun denoting non-human species singular  
> Are part of worldly usage in honour clothed,  
> Not permitted they are by norms of grammar though.
>
> (*Collatikāram : Kiḷaviyākkam*, verse 27)
