# Blossom 76 — Four Sources of Fear

## Demoness (aṇaṅku)

A youth with the resolve to converse only in chaste Tamiḻ, adhering to the tenets of literature and grammar, confided to an elderly person of his hamlet possessing profound knowledge of Tamiḻ, that he would very soon tie the knots. The person greeted him with a heartfelt wish, “May you get an *aṇaṅku* most suited to your life!”

Incidentally, the youth had browsed the dictionary just that day and found out that *aṇaṅku* meant ghost. “Sir, why do you wish me to be tied up with a ghost?” The learned elder explained, “Don’t you know that the dictionary details *aṇaṅku* as ‘a woman’, ‘a ghost’ and a ‘mohini’ (a devil in the guise of a beautiful woman)?” Even in this hi-tech era, there are people horrified by such things as ghost, apparition and black magic. Therefore, in *Tolkāppiyam, aṇaṅku* is shown as a being evoking fear.

## Wild animals (vilaṅku)

Owing to the fear of lion, tiger, bear and other dangerous animals and being unequal to them in strength and valour, the primitive man hunted them with arrows and spears, while his modern counterpart uses bullets. Of course, they do so from a safe distance.
